Recently i published an article with the **[Ultimate List of 1000+ IT4IT Tools 2026](/the-ultimate-list-of-1000-it-management-tools-2026/).** Since then i have updated the categories and subcategories and added the tools to the list that are used for Data Center Management and for the Shared/Corporate Functions such as HR, Risk, Finance, etc. **The List now covers well known tools of/for IT and of/for the Shared Enterprise Functions (e.g. HR, Risk, Finance, etc.); the list currently contains 1,438 COMMODITY IT4ESM tools/platforms.**

The Categories and Subcategories that are used are a mix of TBM and IT4IT naming conventions, and are intentionally chosen.

**By simply marking the applications that are used by the company, in this market-standard list, one avoids having debates/confusion about (sub)categorization of the IT4ESM applications in your landscape, and one can easily identify gaps and duplications.**

I define all IT4ESM tools as Applications since i believe their lifecycle must be managed and corporate policies must apply to them as well. The "Corporate and Shared" Applications go into a Business Application Portfolio, and the IT4IT Tools go into a Technology Mngt Application Portfolio. The "real" Business Applications that are used by/for External Customers (i.e. not included in this IT4ESM list) go into the Business Application Portfolio as well.

Even though one could argue the are IT4IT CI's, I don't register models of Middleware and Infra/OS Software as Business Applications, since it's not practical/efficient to define/maintain and relate instances thereof.

All deployment-specific instances of the IT4ESM Applications, are related to the Application that they are an instance of. Infrastructure CI's are mapped to the instances that depend on them, often using tag-based mapping.

Design-time (functional) dependencies are defined in the application portfolio, and (un)authorized run-time (technical) dependencies are discovered.
