### UPDATE: [IT4IT/IT4ESM Atlas](https://javc.nl/it4it-atlas/) now contains 1600 Platforms

This is an overview of 1136 IT management tools, grouped into 26 categories across 7 domains — Strategy & Planning, Software Engineering & Delivery, IT Service & Asset Management, IT Operations, Security, People, AI — aligned to The Open Group's IT4IT reference architecture and extended to cover every vendor named in the relevant current Gartner Magic Quadrants, plus open-source platforms used at enterprise scale.

### 1) Strategy & Planning

-   **1.1) Enterprise Architecture (EA)** – 32 Tools — Focused on aligning technology strategies with overarching business objectives, facilitating structured frameworks, and ensuring that IT investments support long-term organizational goals.
-   **2.2) Strategic Portfolio Management (SPM) & Project Portfolio Management (PPM)** – 22 Tools — Covers the prioritization, scheduling, and resourcing of IT and business initiatives — from Gartner's Strategic Portfolio Management and Adaptive Project Management & Reporting Magic Quadrants.
-   **1.3) IT Financial Management (ITFM/TBM) & Innovation Managemen**t – 5 Tools — Tracks and optimizes IT spend against business value (Technology Business Management) and manages the intake and evaluation of new ideas and innovation initiatives.

### 2) Software Engineering & Delivery

-   **2.1) IT Development Operations (DevOps)** – 113 Tools — Emphasizes the synergy between software development and operations, streamlining processes such as continuous integration, delivery, and collaboration to accelerate product releases and improve quality.
-   **2.2) Low-Code Application Platforms** – 14 Tools — Enterprise platforms for building custom applications with minimal hand-written code, spanning citizen-developer and professional-developer use cases.
-   **2.3) Robotic Process Automation (RPA) & Business Process Automation** – 9 Tools — Automates repetitive, rules-based digital tasks and business processes, from desktop bots to enterprise-scale orchestration.
-   **2.4) API Management** – 14 Tools — Designs, publishes, secures, and monitors APIs across their full lifecycle, enabling controlled access to services and data.
-   **2.5) Enterprise iPaaS & Integration** – 16 Tools — Connects applications, data, and processes across cloud and on-premises systems through integration-platform-as-a-service and workflow-automation tools.

### 3) IT Service & Asset Management

-   **3.1) IT Service Management (ITSM)** – 71 Tools — Provides best practices and methodologies (e.g., ITIL) to structure and deliver IT services, optimizing incident, problem, and change management to maintain high service quality and customer satisfaction.
-   **3.2) IT Asset Management (ITAM)** – 98 Tools — Dedicated to tracking and optimizing the life cycles of hardware and software assets, ensuring cost effectiveness, compliance, and robust inventory control.
-   **3.3) Unified Endpoint Management (UEM)** – 13 Tools — Centrally manages, configures, and secures desktops, mobile devices, and other endpoints across their lifecycle.

### 4) IT Operations

-   **4.1) Observability Platforms (ITOM)** – 145 Tools — Delivers comprehensive monitoring and management of IT systems, networks, and infrastructures — metrics, logs, traces, and AIOps — helping organizations maintain high performance, reliability, and availability.
-   **4.2) Enterprise Backup & Data Protection / Disaster Recovery** – 21 Tools — Protects enterprise data through backup, replication, and recovery orchestration, including disaster-recovery-as-a-service.

### 5) Security

-   **5.1) Vulnerability & Exposure Management** – 125 Tools — Centers on vulnerability scanning, attack-surface management, and exposure prioritization to safeguard corporate data, networks, and systems from ever-evolving cyber threats.
-   **5.2) Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)** – 18 Tools — Aggregates, correlates, and analyzes security event data across the enterprise to detect and investigate threats.
-   **5.3) Security Orchestration, Automation and Response (SOAR) – 8 Tools** — Automates and orchestrates incident response workflows and threat-intelligence sharing across the security stack.
-   **5.4) Endpoint Protection Platforms (EPP) & Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)** – 15 Tools — Protects, detects, and responds to threats on laptops, servers, and other endpoints.
-   **5.5) Security Service Edge (SSE), SASE & CASB** – 15 Tools — Converges network security functions — secure web gateway, CASB, ZTNA — into a cloud-delivered edge, often paired with SD-WAN as SASE.
-   **5.6) Email Security** – 13 Tools — Defends against phishing, business email compromise, and malicious attachments across enterprise email platforms.
-   **5.7) Network & Hybrid Mesh Firewalls** – 17 Tools — Enforces network segmentation and traffic policy across distributed, hybrid, and cloud environments.
-   **5.8) Identity & Access Management (IAM, IGA & PAM)** – 40 Tools — Manages who and what can access enterprise systems, spanning access management/SSO, identity governance and administration, and privileged access management.
-   **5.9) IT Integrated Risk Management (IRM/GRC)** – 111 Tools — Offers strategies and systems to identify, assess, and mitigate operational and regulatory risks, maintaining compliance while supporting organizational resilience.

### 6) Corporate & Shared

-   \*\*6.1) Workforce (HR)\*\*– 137 Tools — Concentrates on recruitment, workforce planning, and talent development, ensuring that organizations attract, retain, and manage the right personnel.

-   **6.5) Property & Facility**
-   **6.6) Corporate Communication**

### 7) AI

-   **7.1) AI Platforms** – 41 Tools — Covers the foundation-model and enterprise AI/LLM platforms, copilots and agent-orchestration tools, and AI-augmented IT operations layers that increasingly sit on top of — and inside — the other categories.
-   **7.2) Data Science & Machine Learning Platforms** – 12 Tools — Builds, trains, deploys, and governs machine-learning and AI models, including MLOps and self-hosted model-serving infrastructure.
-   **7.3) Enterprise Conversational AI** – 13 Tools — Purpose-built platforms for building virtual agents, chatbots, and voice assistants for customer service and employee support.

These tools collectively represent the multifaceted nature of modern IT management. It is highly unlikely a company will buy/use all 1138 tools. However, it is not unlikely a company will use –and will try to integrate– a hundred of the tools listed below.

For large companies that make use of a lot of technologies of different vendors, the IT Tooling Domain probably is the biggest and most complex Architecture IT Domain they have.

Which tool is best fit for purpose and is best fit for audience, depends on a lot of factors. Some companies let the IT teams select their own tools. Others try to enforce enterprise standards. To simplify the landscape, to avoid tooling overlap, and to maximize economy of scale (e.g. for training and tool licenses), i believe companies should try to use as little tools as possible, whilst choosing the right tools for the jobs at hand. Some things holistically simply make sense to me, and some don’t. E.g. companies should not use multiple Enterprise Architecture Tools side by side, unless if one is the source of truth for the others.
