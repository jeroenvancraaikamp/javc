By regulating working relationships, the government aims to provide more clarity and certainty about the legal position of clients and contractors. By enforcing the DBA Act and introducing the VBAR Act, it seeks to combat false self-employment and ensure that workers remain within the social security system. The ultimate goal is a transparent and fair labour market without abuse of flexibility.

The Dutch government has decided that, as of 1 January 2025, the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst) will once again actively enforce the Deregulation of the Assessment of Employment Relationships Act (Wet Deregulering Beoordeling Arbeidsrelaties, DBA) of May 2016. To make this happen, the Tax Administration has appointed 80 full-time staff. Enforcement of the DBA Act is aimed at clients in the Netherlands, not at contractors, although the DBA affects both. Whether or not contractors are based in the Netherlands makes no difference under the DBA Act, although it is unclear how a client is supposed to pay taxes and contributions for someone who is not known to the Dutch Tax Administration at all.

**Key developments:**

-   The Ministry of Social Affairs and Employment (SZW) has introduced an **[online Employment Relationship Assessment web module](https://beoordelingarbeidsrelatie.nl/)**.
-   SZW has published a bill for the new **"[Clarification of the Assessment of Employment Relationships and Legal Presumption Act](https://wetgevingskalender.overheid.nl/regeling/WGK014517/documenten/Raad%20van%20State/Adviesaanvraag%20aanhangig%20bij%20Raad%20van%20State/1)" (Wet Verduidelijking Beoordeling Arbeidsrelaties en Rechtsvermoeden, VBAR)**.
-   The Tax Administration has drawn up an [**enforcement framework**](https://l.facebook.com/l.php?u=https%3A%2F%2Fdownload.belastingdienst.nl%2Fbelastingdienst%2Fdocs%2Fafwegingskader_dienstbetrekking_lh6301z1pl.pdf%3Ffbclid%3DIwZXh0bgNhZW0CMTEAAR1nlnod6pvFLTQ_5b4xD8h-8HE7H5mTQ8bBA0E_gtsE9eNuK9jHTSc5fa4_aem_HVedEICKSgdhKKQ0oqDSuA&h=AT2ODwkchjZKXHEvKrS84AyGFpfMUYn_EHQkaPWF01naxu8pinUBWi7u_QF8BsUD6QFamkdieVpJEpeNwEnP1pq3CfL3M49ljhwOmFrLoF4A-oHfAoXiYGJsYP-oODgWHgnHADA&__tn__=H-R&c[0]=AT2o0K83WU5M7TuqulJKSiWdw3plDgwt6GSSeYqY9hggq243GaKhfLyDRPd6BlOz3dluPHAlgM_l6m35k9M2IY0LYTrIaju0MNIkBodq7JuFMmJoZPoPuQVKkEMnKo9ETqo5R95_ggL0uDxp5JKF5bbbHx0ENbuBPYqxbcdbuqcyZm9fd4RHpZ9pYvU2_u5Rib_RE1EUIgOVU0LozB8zZwhgv9KfRw) describing how it will enforce the DBA Act from 1 January 2025.
-   In a number of [**motions**](https://www.tweedekamer.nl/kamerstukken/stemmingsuitslagen/detail?id=2024P14922&did=2024P14922), the House of Representatives (Tweede Kamer) has determined, among other things, that enforcement of the DBA Act will not be fully digital and will not immediately involve strict fines.

The introduction of the VBAR Act is planned for 1 January 2026, provided it is approved by the Senate and the House of Representatives. In 2025, the DBA Act will continue to be enforced.

According to the enforcement framework, the Tax Administration will already use the web module as a guideline for enforcing the DBA Act in 2025. The enforcement framework also refers to important case law, such as the Deliveroo ruling, in which the Supreme Court (Hoge Raad) decided that Deliveroo couriers had to be regarded as employees, despite an approved model agreement.

**Factors that determine the nature of the working relationship**

The nature of the working relationship between client and contractor is determined by factors including the following:

1.  **Personal performance of work:** The contractor's obligation to carry out the work personally. If the contractor can be replaced by someone else without permission, an employment relationship is less likely.
2.  **Relationship of authority:** The extent to which the client gives instructions on how the work is to be carried out. Where there is a clear relationship of authority, an employment relationship is more likely.
3.  **Pay:** The payment for the work performed. Regular payments may indicate an employment relationship.
4.  **Embedding in the organisation:** If the contractor's work is integrated into the client's regular business operations, this may indicate an employment relationship, for example when the contractor performs the same work as regular employees.
5.  **Entrepreneurship and own risk:** A genuinely self-employed person bears entrepreneurial risk, such as having multiple clients, setting their own rates and bearing financial risks. The absence of these may indicate an employment relationship.
6.  **Duration and continuity of the relationship:** A long-term and exclusive collaboration between client and contractor may indicate an employment relationship. Having multiple clients and carrying out short-term assignments points more towards self-employment.
7.  **Freedom in carrying out the work:** A contractor who has complete freedom in how they carry out the work, without detailed instructions from the client, is more likely to be regarded as self-employed. Strict instructions and supervision may indicate a relationship of authority.
8.  **Freedom to accept assignments:** A self-employed person generally has the freedom to turn down assignments. If a contractor is obliged to accept assignments from the client, this may indicate an employment relationship.
9.  **Use of own materials and tools:** A self-employed person often uses their own tools or materials. When the client provides these, this may indicate a relationship of authority.
10.  **Responsibility for the end result:** A contractor who is responsible for the end result and liable for any errors is more likely to be regarded as self-employed. For employees, this responsibility generally lies with the employer.

It is essential to assess the full context of the working relationship, as it is the combination of these factors that determines whether there is an employment relationship or self-employment.

**Soft landing approach and false self-employment**

The House of Representatives has called for a gradual transition. This means that the Tax Administration may not immediately impose fines on clients when false self-employment is suspected. In addition, parties will be given room to adjust their relationship so that it can be regarded as a purely business relationship.

Clients and contractors are jointly responsible for complying with the legal guidelines. The web module provides clarity about the nature of the working relationship. If a contractor can be classified as self-employed, the client does not need to pay taxes and contributions. However, if the contractor is regarded as an employee, the client must take action, for example by putting the contractor on the payroll or adjusting the relationship. In cases of doubt, the Tax Administration will assess the situation and issue a final ruling.

**Assessment of working relationships and entrepreneurship**

Contractors who work for a client and for whom the web module or the enforcement framework points to an employment relationship will be regarded by the Tax Administration as employees of the client. Whether a contractor also qualifies as an entrepreneur for income tax and VAT purposes depends on additional tax criteria, such as spending at least 1,225 hours per year on the business.

Contractors who work through an employment or secondment agency are formally employed by the agency and not by the client. In this case, the agency pays the taxes and contributions.

Contractors who carry out assignments through their own private limited company (BV) are, in principle, not regarded as employees of the client. When the VBAR Act is introduced in 2026, these contractors will also have to comply with the new rules. For these arrangements, the web module does not yet provide a definitive answer on false self-employment.

**Arrangements and liability**

The enforcement framework states that contractors who use arrangements to circumvent the DBA Act will no longer be regarded as a business from 1 January 2025. One example is the director and majority shareholder (DGA) who hires themselves out through their own BV, in which case the Tax Administration may conclude that there is false self-employment. In the future, the VBAR Act and the web module will provide more clarity about the role of DGAs and other arrangements.

**Fines and enforcement**

Clients who take demonstrable measures to reduce false self-employment will probably not receive high fines in 2025. Fines and additional tax assessments are mainly expected for clients who knowingly encourage false self-employment or take no measures to combat it.

When the VBAR Act comes into force on 1 January 2026, the rules will be strictly enforced and the new frameworks will apply in full.
